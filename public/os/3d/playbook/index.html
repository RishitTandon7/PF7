Brief in.
Direction out.

The designer tool turns a short design brief, or a reference image, into three things a designer can use straight away: style keywords, CMF and material notes, and five render prompts. This page is the manual.

What it does

  • Style keywords. Eight short phrases that name the feeling of the product, so a team can agree on direction in one glance.
  • CMF and material notes. Colour, material and finish suggestions for the main surfaces, each with a one-line reason.
  • Render prompts. Five prompts for an image generator: studio shot, exploded view, in-context scene, CMF board and a sketch sheet.

When to use it

  • At the start of a project, to get from a paragraph of words to a first visual direction in minutes.
  • Before a moodboard session, to give the team a shared vocabulary.
  • When you need quick CMF starting points to react to, not to approve.

The five steps

  1. 01

    Write the brief

    Two to five sentences. Say what the product is, who uses it, the mood you want, and one hard constraint (for example: must be readable in direct sunlight).

  2. 02

    Add a reference (optional)

    Drop a JPG, PNG or WebP of a product, a material or a moodboard frame. It is resized in your browser before it is sent.

  3. 03

    Generate

    Press Generate. Results typically take a few seconds. If the live model is unavailable you get a clearly labelled sample result instead of an error.

  4. 04

    Read the three outputs

    Start with the keywords, check the CMF notes against your constraint, then copy the prompts you want into your image tool.

  5. 05

    Iterate

    Change one thing in the brief (the mood, the material, the user) and run it again. Small edits to the brief move the output more than long rewrites.

Tips

  • Name the user and the setting. "Commuter in a hot, bright city" beats "modern and sleek".
  • Use opposites to steer: "calm but not boring", "premium but not shiny".
  • Keep one accent colour in mind. The tool defaults to a single accent so the result stays disciplined.
  • Edit the prompts before you use them. They are a first draft written for a general image model.

Limitations

  • Suggestions are starting points. Material and finish ideas still need checking for cost, manufacturability and regulation.
  • The image input is read for look and feel only. It does not measure dimensions or identify a brand.
  • Do not paste confidential briefs. Text is sent to a third-party language model to generate the result.
  • The tool is rate-limited (a handful of runs per ten minutes per visitor) to keep it free to use.
